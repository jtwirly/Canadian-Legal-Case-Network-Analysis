# SCC 2000-2026 Bibliographic Analysis

<img width="3581" height="4542" alt="scc_biblio_analysis" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/196272a3-58d9-4582-83c4-8ddcc77b7926" />

Five panels, three distinct bibliographic methods:

**Co-citation community structure (top)** — 792 cases, 5,546 links, 12 communities detected by greedy modularity. The clustering is remarkably clean and jurisprudentially coherent without any manual labelling: the algorithm recovered admin law (*Dunsmuir* era), constitutional/Charter, criminal procedure, s.8 search and seizure, federalism/division of powers, civil procedure, and Aboriginal rights as distinct doctrinal neighbourhoods. That's a validation that citation behaviour tracks doctrine.

**Bibliographic coupling (mid-left)** — Cases linked by ≥8 shared references — meaning they drew from the same intellectual raw material. The standout finding from the data: *Vavilov* (2019) is bibliographically coupled to 26 cases, the highest in the corpus. It shares references with everything from *Dunsmuir* to Charter cases to federalism decisions — which reflects how explicitly the majority surveyed and synthesized the entire admin law canon before rewriting it.

**Doctrinal bridge cases (mid-right)** — Betweenness centrality in the co-citation graph identifies cases that *connect* clusters rather than dominate within one. *Housen v. Nikolaisen* (2002) is the dominant bridge by a wide margin — appellate standards of review are applied across criminal, civil, administrative, and constitutional appeals, so it appears in every cluster's neighbourhood. *Bell ExpressVu* (statutory interpretation canon) is second for the same reason. *R. v. Jordan* (trial delay) and *Vavilov* are the two modern-era bridges.

**Doctrinal integration by decade (bottom-left)** — The ratio of within-cluster to cross-cluster co-citation links shifts across decades. The 2000s are more siloed; the 2020s show proportionally more cross-cluster links, consistent with the SCC increasingly drawing on administrative law principles in criminal cases (*Jordan* uses reasonableness-adjacent reasoning) and Charter principles in commercial disputes.

**Co-citation heatmap (bottom-right)** — The *Dunsmuir*–*Khosa*–*Agraira* triad lights up as a dense sub-block — those three are almost always cited together in admin law decisions. *Housen* co-occurs with nearly everything. *Bedford* and *Carter* are tightly coupled (31 co-citations) — the assisted dying line is always cited as a pair.
